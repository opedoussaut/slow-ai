THE INTELLIGENCE ALLOCATION CHALLENGE

Think first.
AI when it matters.

Most systems ask which AI model? ƧlowAI asks a better question: what is the minimum intelligence required for this decision? Start by curating evidence, then use deterministic software, a tiny decision model, specialized AI, or frontier reasoning only when justified.

01 · Curate before you reason

Thousands of raw signals should not become thousands of tokens. Deterministic processing filters, correlates and validates data, then keeps only decision-relevant evidence — with links back to the originals.

02 · Route each decision to the right intelligence

Four alternative routes. They are not four stages every request must pass through. Escalate only when the task, uncertainty, risk or evidence demands it.

ROUTE A

No AI

Known formulas, rules, thresholds, database lookups and workflows. Exact, traceable, repeatable.

ROUTE B

Decision model

Compact learned or probabilistic decision logic for bounded, typed questions and calibrated uncertainty.

ROUTE C

Specialized AI

Task-trained perception or prediction models when rules and small decision models cannot handle the patterns.

ROUTE D

Frontier AI

Novel, ambiguous, multi-domain reasoning where deeper synthesis is justified — with constraints and human oversight.

04 · Close the loop with evidence

Compare correctness, coverage, latency, reliability, cost, measured energy, and operational risk. Keep missing measurements explicitly unknown. Require human approval before consequential actions.

See the first real published experiment

The Steel Plates Faults benchmark compares a deliberately simple majority-class baseline with a classical 3-nearest-neighbor model on 388 held-out cases. This supports a narrow classification comparison; it does not validate the four-route cooling-loop architecture or demonstrate energy savings.
